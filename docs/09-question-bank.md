# 09 — Nomination Question Bank

One nomination question per round, drawn automatically from this bank so a
4-round event surfaces four different traits. Facilitator-editable per event.

## Why rotating beats repeating

Asking "who was the MVP?" four times measures one thing four times, and that thing
is largely **conspicuousness** — the loud, the funny, and the confident get picked
repeatedly while the person who quietly did the coordination is invisible all day.

Rotating the question changes what the spotlight is pointed at. "Who made sure
everyone got a say?" and "who kept going when it got frustrating?" find different
people than "who took charge?" does. Over four rounds you sample four different
kinds of good, and far more of the room gets named at least once — which is both
better data and a better experience for participants.

This also **improves** the statistics rather than costing them. Each individual
question ends up with only one round of data per person, so no single question can
carry a ranking. But the *aggregate* — total nominations received across all
questions, normalised by rounds played — becomes a broader and fairer measure:
"stood out on some positive dimension," rather than "was the most visible." See
[04](04-scoring-and-bias.md#nominations-mvp-questions).

## Trait tags

Every question carries a tag, so nominations aggregate into three interpretable
dimensions over the event. These map onto what your clients say they want —
potential leaders and outstanding workers — without ever producing a ranking.

| Tag | Reads as | Client language |
|---|---|---|
| `LEAD` | Direction, decisions, taking responsibility | Leadership potential |
| `WORK` | Effort, problem-solving, follow-through | Outstanding contributor |
| `SOCIAL` | Inclusion, morale, cohesion | Team glue / culture carrier |

A person with 3 `LEAD` nominations across 4 rounds is a very different signal from
one with 3 `SOCIAL` nominations, and both are worth telling a client about.
Neither is a score.

## The bank

| # | Question | Tag | Finds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who helped the team decide what to do? | `LEAD` | Directive leadership — the obvious one |
| 2 | Who made sure everyone got a say? | `LEAD` | Facilitative leadership — usually a *different* person to #1 |
| 3 | Who took responsibility when things went wrong? | `LEAD` | Accountability under pressure |
| 4 | Who kept the team on track when it drifted? | `LEAD` | Process discipline; often the quiet organiser |
| 5 | Who came up with the idea that worked? | `WORK` | Insight — rewards the person who thought, not the one who spoke |
| 6 | Who put in the most effort? | `WORK` | Raw contribution |
| 7 | Who kept going when it got frustrating? | `WORK` | Persistence; finds resilience, not charisma |
| 8 | Who did the unglamorous work nobody else wanted? | `WORK` | The single best question in this bank for finding invisible contributors |
| 9 | Who explained things clearly when others were stuck? | `WORK` | Teaching and communication |
| 10 | Who noticed something the rest of us missed? | `WORK` | Attention to detail |
| 11 | Who made the team more fun? | `SOCIAL` | Energy and morale |
| 12 | Who did you enjoy working with most? | `SOCIAL` | General affiliation — the closest to a popularity question, use sparingly |
| 13 | Who stayed calm when the team got stressed? | `SOCIAL` | Emotional steadiness |
| 14 | Who encouraged someone who was struggling? | `SOCIAL` | Support — very often names someone no other question will |
| 15 | Who listened best? | `SOCIAL` | Rewards the opposite of loudness by construction |
| 16 | Who surprised you today? | `SOCIAL` | Deliberately open; good final round, and a lovely thing to be told |

## Selection rules

Encoded in the app, not left to the facilitator to remember:

1. **One tag per round, no tag twice in a row.** A 3-round event gets one of each;
   a 4-round event gets a repeat of whichever tag the client cares about most,
   set at event setup.
2. **Question 1 or 2 in the first round.** They are the most legible, and the
   first round is when people understand the task least.
3. **Question 8 or 14 no later than the third round.** These are the ones that
   find people nothing else finds, and they work better once teammates have seen
   each other work.
4. **Question 16 last** where the round count allows. It is the best note to end
   an event on.
5. **Never two questions from the same tag in the same event** unless the round
   count exceeds the number of tags.
6. Seeded, so the sequence is reproducible and explicable.

## Wording rules for anything you add

- **Positive framing only.** There is no "who contributed least" and there never
  will be. A negative nomination question in a room of colleagues creates a
  problem you cannot take back, and it is not something this app will support.
- **Behaviour, not character.** "Who explained things clearly" is observable.
  "Who was the smartest" is a judgement about a person and invites bias.
- **One behaviour per question.** "Who led and motivated the team" is two
  questions, and nobody knows which one they answered.
- **Answerable by everyone in the team.** If only the person who happened to sit
  next to someone could know, the question measures seating, not behaviour.
- **Short enough to read on a 320 px screen** without scrolling — roughly eight
  words.

## Skip handling

Every question allows "no one in particular." Forcing a nomination from someone
who genuinely didn't observe the behaviour manufactures noise, and the normalised
index in [04](04-scoring-and-bias.md) handles the missing data correctly. Expect
a 10–20% skip rate on the subtler questions; that is healthy, not a defect.
